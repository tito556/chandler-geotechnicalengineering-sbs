The rig rolls in early, before the desert heat builds—a CME-75 or Diedrich drill with a 140-pound safety hammer dropping 30 inches per blow on the drive head. In Chandler, where hard caliche sits under loose alluvium, that standard setup takes a beating. Our team runs split-spoon sampling per ASTM D1586 in subdivisions from Ocotillo to Sun Groves, pulling N-values every 5 feet through sandy loam, then rock-hard calcium carbonate layers that push refusal above 50 blows. The data feeds bearing capacity design for slabs and shallow footings across the East Valley, where soil can shift from sand to cemented conglomerate in less than 3 vertical feet. We log every run with USCS classification on the spot, so the geotechnical engineer gets a continuous profile without guessing what’s beneath the next spoon. For deeper refusal zones where SPT stops making sense, we often pair the work with a CPT rig to get continuous tip resistance through the hardpan.

Chandler’s caliche layers can generate refusal at 50 blows in 6 inches—that’s not rock. It’s cemented alluvium, and it still requires a design decision.

How we work

Soil conditions between West Chandler and the Price Corridor flip fast. Near Kyrene, you get coarse alluvial sand with gravel lenses—drains well, N-values in the 20s, straightforward for spread footings sized with a 2,000 psf presumptive bearing. Cross the 101 toward the old farm blocks south of Germann Road, and you hit silty clay with occasional organics from the historic irrigation tailwater ponds, where blow counts drop to 8 or 10 and the moisture reads close to the plastic limit. That contrast matters when a structural engineer is sizing a tilt-up warehouse slab on one side of town versus a two-story medical office on the other. We’ve correlated Chandler’s typical alluvial fan stratigraphy with hundreds of lab-tested split-spoon samples, so when a client needs Atterberg limits run on a clay seam at 12 feet, we can predict the PI range before the jar even leaves the field. The same profile often benefits from grain-size analysis on the sand lenses to confirm drainage potential for retention basin design.

Local geotechnical context

The Queen Creek floodplain and the Salt River alluvial fan both influence Chandler’s subsurface, placing much of the city in Site Class D by ASCE 7 criteria. Loose sands between 10 and 20 feet depth can trigger liquefaction concerns under a 2,500-year seismic event, particularly near the Loop 202 corridor where the water table sits as shallow as 15 feet in wet years. We run N1(60) corrections on every SPT blow count in saturated zones, applying the Youd-Idriss 2001 procedure to flag potentially liquefiable layers before the structural design locks in. The caliche cap that gives good bearing can also mask loose material underneath—a risk that only shows up when the driller’s log reads refusal at 8 feet and the owner assumes the whole column is dense. Skipping SPT depth through refusal without probing the next 10 feet has caused differential settlement in Chandler tilt-up buildings, a problem that a few extra spoons would have caught.

Questions and answers

How much does an SPT borehole cost in Chandler?

For a standard SPT borehole in Chandler to 25 feet depth with split-spoon sampling every 5 feet, typical cost runs between US$530 and US$770 per hole. The price varies with total depth, number of holes on the same mobilization, and whether caliche layers require extended drilling time. A two-hole package on a standard lot usually falls near the middle of that range.

How many SPT boreholes does IBC require for a commercial building in Chandler?

IBC Table 1803.1 sets minimums based on building footprint, but Chandler’s alluvial fan geology usually demands at least one borehole per 2,500 square feet for structures under 5,000 square feet, with at least two holes minimum. Spacing tightens when the preliminary log shows rapid lateral variation in caliche depth or loose sand layers, which is common east of Arizona Avenue.

What depth should SPT boreholes reach for a slab-on-grade warehouse in the Chandler Airpark area?

For a slab-on-grade warehouse near the Chandler Municipal Airport, we typically drill to 25 feet minimum, which is 1.5 times the width of the largest spread footing plus depth to competent bearing. The caliche cap in that area often starts between 6 and 10 feet, but we probe at least 10 feet below the base of the deepest footing to check for loose sand under the cemented layer.

Can SPT refusal in caliche be used to justify higher bearing capacity?

Yes, but with caution. Refusal at 50 blows per 6 inches in Chandler’s caliche often corresponds to a cemented soil that can support 4,000 to 6,000 psf in bearing, but the driller must log exactly where refusal occurred and whether it’s a thin crust or a thick layer. We recommend probing at least 5 feet into the refusal zone with a rock core setup if the design calls for bearing above 3,000 psf, because thin caliche can fracture under load and punch into softer material below.
