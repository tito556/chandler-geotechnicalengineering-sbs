The drilling rig positions itself over the staked point while the split-spoon sampler is readied for the first blow. The hollow-stem auger starts rotating, cutting through the upper crust of the Sonoran Desert floor. In Chandler, where summer surface temperatures exceed 140°F before noon, the field crew works with calibrated equipment and strict adherence to ASTM D1586 procedures. We recover disturbed and undisturbed samples from depths that typically range between 10 and 40 feet, depending on the target bearing stratum. The recovered cores are immediately sealed, labeled, and transported to our accredited laboratory for classification, strength, and consolidation testing. This is the raw material of a proper soil mechanics study: numbers that replace assumptions with verifiable behavior. For projects near the Price Road Corridor or south of the Loop 202, where stratigraphy shifts within a few hundred feet, we often combine the standard penetration test with a CPT test to obtain a continuous profile of tip resistance and sleeve friction without losing the thin silt seams that can control settlement.

Chandler soils can lose more than half their bearing capacity when moisture content rises just 4% above the optimum; the difference between a stable slab and a call-back is measured in compaction percentage points.

How we work

Chandler’s transformation from agricultural grid to high-tech manufacturing hub compressed decades of urban development into a few boom cycles. The original irrigation network left behind a patchwork of alluvial deposits, redistributed silts, and pockets of uncontrolled fill that still surprise contractors today. A soil mechanics study in this city must reconcile the legacy of leveled cotton fields with the demands of modern tilt-up concrete warehouses and multi-story mixed-use structures. Our laboratory program starts with visual-manual classification per ASTM D2487, then moves to mechanical sieve analysis, Atterberg limits, and moisture-density relationships. For sites east of Arizona Avenue, where the clay fraction increases noticeably, we run swell-consolidation tests to quantify heave potential under post-construction moisture conditions. The interplay between desert pavement, caliche lenses, and deeper cemented sands means that presumptive bearing values from generic tables are unreliable. We complement the index testing with triaxial consolidated-undrained shear strength measurements when the structural engineer needs drained and undrained parameters for slope stability or retaining wall design.

Local geotechnical context

IBC Section 1803 and ASCE 7-22 mandate geotechnical investigations for all structures in Seismic Design Category D, which covers most of Maricopa County including Chandler. The hazard here is not just seismic shaking but the combination of moderate seismicity with moisture-sensitive soils. Collapsible silty sands, common in the basin-fill deposits west of the Loop 101, can undergo sudden volume reduction upon wetting, with settlement exceeding 6 inches under foundation loads. The risk is compounded in Chandler because landscape irrigation and stormwater retention basins alter the desert moisture regime permanently after construction. A soil mechanics study that skips collapse potential evaluation or misidentifies a low-plasticity silt as a sandy lean clay may lead to differential movement that cracks structural slabs, tilts column bases, and shears utility connections. The financial exposure from remedial underpinning or slab jacking easily reaches six figures on a commercial building, not counting operational downtime. Our report includes specific recommendations for moisture conditioning, overexcavation depths, and post-compaction verification using nuclear density gauges.

Regulatory framework

ASTM D1586-18 establishes the procedure for the Standard Penetration Test; ASTM D2487-17 defines the classification system for soils in engineering applications; ASTM D4767-11(2020) outlines the method for the consolidated undrained triaxial compression test; IBC 2024 Section 1803 specifies requirements for geotechnical investigations; and ASCE 7-22 Chapter 11 provides criteria for seismic design.

Other technical services

01

Foundation Design Soil Characterization

Borehole drilling with SPT sampling at 2.5- to 5-foot intervals, laboratory index testing, unconfined compression on cohesive samples, and bearing capacity calculations for shallow footings or mat foundations. Includes modulus of subgrade reaction for slab-on-grade design.

02

Earthwork and Compaction Control Testing

Proctor curves for on-site and borrow materials, field density testing using nuclear gauge (ASTM D6938), and laboratory CBR when subgrade stiffness is critical. We provide lift-by-lift documentation for building pad certification.

03

Slope Stability and Retaining Wall Analysis

Triaxial shear testing with pore pressure measurement, consolidation parameters, and limit-equilibrium analysis using Spencer or Morgenstern-Price methods. For walls exceeding 6 feet, we provide active, at-rest, and passive earth pressure coefficients per Rankine or Coulomb theory.

Questions and answers

How many borings does the City of Chandler require for a commercial building permit?

The City of Chandler adopts IBC Table 1803.1 as a minimum, which typically calls for at least one boring per 2,500 square feet of building footprint for structures under 100,000 square feet, with a minimum of two borings. However, the geotechnical engineer of record may adjust the number and depth based on subsurface variability encountered. For sites with known fill or collapsible soils, additional borings are often prudent to define the lateral extent of problematic zones.

What is the typical turnaround time from drilling to final soil mechanics report?

Field drilling and sampling usually take one to two days for a standard commercial lot. Laboratory testing requires 7 to 10 business days for index properties, and up to 14 days when consolidation or triaxial tests are included. The final geotechnical report with foundation recommendations, earthwork specifications, and seismic site class determination is typically delivered within three weeks of field completion. We can expedite certain tests for an additional fee when construction schedules demand it.

Does Chandler have special requirements for expansive or collapsible soils?

Yes. The City of Chandler follows IBC Section 1803.5.3, which requires identification of expansive and collapsible soils. When laboratory tests confirm swell potential above 1% or collapse potential under wetting, the geotechnical report must prescribe mitigation measures. Common solutions in Chandler include moisture conditioning to near-optimum, overexcavation and recompaction of the upper 3 to 5 feet, chemical stabilization with lime or cement, and deepened footings extending below the active zone of moisture fluctuation.
