Chandler started as a quiet agricultural outpost in the Salt River Valley, and few people realize how much the underlying soil structure varies across the city. The transition from historic alfalfa fields to high-tech manufacturing campuses and dense residential subdivisions placed new demands on geotechnical design. In our experience, the most efficient way to characterize these sites without disturbing sensitive desert soils is the MASW survey. We see contractors and structural engineers specifying seismic site class parameters earlier in the design phase, and a well-executed VS30 profile gives them exactly that. Chandler sits on a mix of alluvial fan deposits and fine-grained basin fill, so shear wave velocity can shift sharply within a single block. A proper MASW survey maps those contrasts and delivers the data needed for IBC-compliant foundation design.

A single MASW transect in Chandler can reveal a two-class jump in site classification within 200 feet, something no default assumption would ever catch.

Methodology and scope

ASCE 7-22 and the 2021 IBC require a site-specific shear wave velocity profile for any project where the default site class assumption would be conservative or uncertain. In Chandler that uncertainty is real. The near-surface sediments across the Price Corridor and the Kyrene area often contain interbedded silty sands and stiff clays that do not fit cleanly into the code’s generic Site Class D profile. We combine surface-wave acquisition with a 24-channel seismograph and 4.5 Hz geophones, then invert the dispersion curve to extract a layered VS model. The result is a VS30 value tied directly to the ASCE 7 site class table. For deeper basin response, we pair MASW with our seismic refraction crew to constrain bedrock depth and P-wave velocities, which helps when the structural engineer needs to assess basin amplification effects beyond the 30-meter average.

Local considerations

Chandler sits at roughly 1,200 feet elevation on the western flank of the Basin and Range province, and the 2023 USGS National Seismic Hazard Model reflects what we have observed in the field: moderate seismicity combined with soft basin sediments can amplify ground motion significantly. The biggest risk we see is a structural design based on an assumed Site Class D where the measured VS30 drops below 180 m/s, which would push the site into Class E or F territory. That changes the design spectral accelerations and can trigger expensive foundation redesigns if caught late. We also encounter loose unsaturated sands near the surface in the southern sections of the city, and while MASW does not directly measure liquefaction susceptibility, a low VS in the upper 10 meters is a red flag that prompts additional investigation. Integrating the VS profile with liquefaction assessment based on SPT or CPT data clarifies the true risk before the structural drawings are finalized.

Combined Seismic Refraction and MASW Survey

For sites requiring both P-wave and S-wave velocity models, we run coincident seismic refraction and MASW lines. This combination is frequently specified for tilt-up concrete buildings and data centers in the Chandler Airpark area.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a MASW survey take on a typical Chandler commercial lot?

On a standard 2- to 5-acre commercial parcel we usually complete field acquisition in half a day. That covers two to four transect lines with multiple shot points per spread. If the site has heavy caliche near the surface, we may need a few extra minutes per spread to ensure good geophone coupling. Data processing and reporting add another two to three business days.

What does a MASW survey cost for a single-family residential project in Chandler?

For a single-family lot requiring one or two MASW transects to determine the site class, the cost typically ranges from US$1,600 to US$2,740 depending on access conditions and the number of spreads needed. We provide a fixed-price proposal after reviewing the site plan and any available geotechnical data.

Can MASW replace soil borings for site classification?

MASW measures shear wave velocity directly, which is the preferred parameter for ASCE 7 site classification. It does not provide soil samples for laboratory testing however. Most projects in Chandler pair one or two exploratory borings for index testing and visual classification with a MASW survey to refine the VS30 and site class. The combination satisfies both the structural engineer and the geotechnical reviewer at the City of Chandler Building Division.

How does the caliche layer in Chandler affect MASW results?

Caliche, or pedogenic calcium carbonate, is common across Chandler at depths from two to eight feet. It creates a high-velocity near-surface layer that actually improves the dispersion curve quality, because the stiff cap couples seismic energy efficiently into the ground. We account for the velocity inversion in the inversion modeling so the VS30 is not artificially elevated by a thin rigid crust.
