IBC Section 1803 requires a site-specific investigation before placing any shallow foundation. In Chandler, that requirement carries extra weight. The southeast valley floor conceals pockets of highly expansive clay that can swell 10% or more with seasonal moisture changes—enough to lift a lightly loaded footing and crack the superstructure. Our team runs SPT borings to refusal and classifies each layer per ASTM D2487, then delivers a bearing capacity recommendation that satisfies the City of Chandler building official on the first submittal. For sites near the Santan Freeway corridor where deeper granular lenses appear, we often tie the investigation to CPT soundings to map continuous strength profiles without losing the fines signature.

A shallow foundation in Chandler succeeds or fails in the first 24 inches of subgrade—know the swell potential before you pour.

Methodology and scope

The most common mistake we see in Chandler subdivisions is the contractor who treats the entire lot as uniform native soil. A single mass grading report from 2003 does not tell you what is under the northeast corner of your pad today. Expansive clay can be absent at one end of the slab and three feet thick at the other. We catch that discontinuity by pairing laboratory Atterberg limits with in-situ density verification on the compacted subgrade. Then we run a Proctor curve on the same material so the compaction spec matches the soil that is actually there. The result is a shallow foundation design that specifies the right reinforcement, the right thickened-edge depth, and the right presaturation protocol—not a generic detail copied from a Maricopa County standard plate.

Local considerations

Look at the difference between a site west of Arizona Avenue and one inside the Ocotillo area. West-side parcels often sit on older alluvium with a cemented caliche crust that limits water infiltration and keeps clay shrinkage moderate. Ocotillo lots, by contrast, can have deeper collapsible silts over expansive clay—two very different failure modes stacked on top of each other. Without a shallow foundation design that accounts for both the collapsible upper stratum and the swell-prone lower layer, you get differential heave in the first monsoon season. That shows up as drywall cracks, sticking doors, or slab-edge spalling that costs five figures to repair. The design fee is a fraction of that number and it buys you a subgrade that stays within the tolerable total settlement of 1 inch for spread footings.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a shallow foundation investigation take for a typical Chandler residential lot?

Fieldwork usually takes one day for a single-family lot with two borings. The lab program—Atterberg limits, gradation, Proctor, and swell-collapse potential—adds five to seven business days. You should budget two calendar weeks from mobilization to final report submittal.

What does a shallow foundation design investigation cost in Chandler?

For a standard residential lot requiring two borings to 15 feet, lab classification, swell testing, and a sealed bearing capacity report, the fee ranges from US$1,770 to US$3,080 depending on access conditions and the number of swell-collapse specimens requested.

Can I use the Maricopa County prescriptive footing detail instead of a site-specific design?

The County standard detail is a minimum requirement, not a performance guarantee. If your lot contains expansive clay with a PI above 15—common in Chandler—the prescriptive detail may not prevent edge heave or differential movement. A site-specific shallow foundation design tailored to your soil profile gives the building official the documentation required under IBC 1803 and protects you against costly post-construction repairs.
