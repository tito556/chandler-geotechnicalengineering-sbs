In Chandler, pavement distress often begins where the naked eye can't see it—in the subgrade. The city sits on deep alluvial deposits and pockets of clay-rich basin fill that swell with monsoon moisture and shrink under the relentless summer heat. When the mercury pushes past 110°F and afternoon storms saturate the ground in minutes, the pavement structure undergoes stresses that generic designs simply can't handle. That's why we don't start with asphalt thickness; we start with a forensic look at the soil. A proper flexible pavement design here must account for resilient modulus variability across seasons, not just a single snapshot test. Our approach integrates CBR road testing to calibrate the subgrade strength and grain-size analysis to identify the fines content that drives moisture sensitivity. The goal is a pavement section that breathes with Chandler's climate rather than fighting it, ensuring the surface stays smooth from the Price Road Corridor to the residential loops near the Intel campus.

In the Sonoran Desert, a pavement's service life is decided not by the asphalt but by the moisture-structure interaction occurring 18 inches below it.

How we work

A recent project near the Santan Freeway illustrated the typical challenge: a commercial parking lot built on silty sand with intermittent clay lenses. The initial pavement design, based on a single springtime CBR value of 12%, failed after two monsoon seasons with extensive alligator cracking. The forensic investigation revealed that the clay lenses, when saturated, dropped the in-situ CBR to below 4%. Our flexible pavement design addressed this through a three-tier solution: chemical stabilization of the upper 12 inches of subgrade with lime to reduce plasticity, a geogrid-reinforced aggregate base course to bridge differential movement, and a Superpave asphalt mix with a PG 70-10 binder selected for Chandler's high-temperature rutting resistance. The structural number was verified using AASHTO 93 layer coefficients, and the fatigue and rutting performance was modeled with AASHTOWare Pavement ME using local climate data from the Chandler Municipal Airport station. We also incorporated Proctor tests during construction to confirm that the stabilized subgrade achieved 95% of maximum dry density, a critical step that many contractors overlook until the pavement begins to deform under traffic loads.

Local geotechnical context

The nuclear density gauge is the first piece of equipment we roll onto a Chandler pavement job—before the paver, before the rollers. It's not glamorous, but it's the tool that tells the truth about what's happening beneath the lift. The biggest risk in flexible pavement design here isn't calculating the wrong structural number; it's the assumption that the subgrade will stay at the moisture content and density it had on compaction day. The Sonoran Desert's flash-flood hydrology creates perched water tables in low-lying areas, and even well-drained alluvial fans can develop capillary rise that softens the subgrade over time. We see this repeatedly along the Loop 202 corridor, where pavement sections designed without internal drainage layers develop fatigue cracking within five to eight years. A pavement design that ignores Chandler's monsoonal cycle is a liability waiting to happen. The solution lies in designing the pavement as a hydraulic system—incorporating permeable base layers, edge drains, and moisture-conditioned subgrade design values that reflect the equilibrium suction of the local soil. Without this, any asphalt thickness calculation is just a number.

Other technical services

01

Pavement Structural Design (AASHTO 93 & MEPDG)

Full layer thickness design using AASHTO 93 and Mechanistic-Empirical Pavement Design Guide (MEPDG) calibrated for Arizona's climate. We determine the optimal asphalt, base, and subbase thicknesses based on projected ESALs, subgrade Mr, and local material properties.

02

Subgrade Stabilization & Treatment Design

Chemical stabilization protocols using lime or cement for Chandler's expansive clay subgrades. We specify treatment depths, application rates, and compaction criteria based on laboratory mix designs and pH testing to achieve long-term volume stability.

03

Construction QA/QC & Forensic Evaluation

On-site density testing, asphalt coring, and falling weight deflectometer (FWD) analysis to verify design assumptions during and after construction. Forensic investigations for failed pavements include trenching, layer modulus back-calculation, and root cause analysis.

Questions and answers

What is the typical cost range for a flexible pavement design in Chandler?

A complete flexible pavement design package for a commercial or residential project in Chandler typically ranges from US$1,440 to US$4,880. The final cost depends on the project's traffic classification, the extent of subgrade investigation required, and whether the design follows AASHTO 93 or the more detailed MEPDG approach, which requires additional laboratory testing for resilient modulus and climate modeling.

Why does Chandler's climate require a different pavement design than Phoenix or Tucson?

While all three cities share the Sonoran Desert climate, Chandler's location on the Gila River alluvial plain creates specific subsurface conditions: higher silt content in the upper soil horizons, shallower groundwater in some corridors, and more intense localized monsoon rainfall due to orographic effects from the Santan Mountains. These factors demand a flexible pavement design with enhanced drainage features and subgrade moisture conditioning that might not be necessary in coarser-grained Phoenix basin soils.

How do you determine the right asphalt binder grade for Chandler's temperatures?

We use the Superpave performance grading system based on Chandler's historical pavement temperature data. The high-temperature grade is selected to resist rutting at the seven-day maximum pavement temperature, which in Chandler can exceed 150°F at the surface. The low-temperature grade accounts for the occasional winter freeze. For most arterial roads and commercial lots, a PG 70-10 binder is the baseline; for heavily loaded truck routes and intersections, we often specify PG 76-10 to handle the combination of high heat and standing loads.
