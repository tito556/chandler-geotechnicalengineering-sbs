A full-column sieve stack coupled with a 152H hydrometer setup defines every particle distribution project in Chandler. The lab configures eight-inch brass sieves from No. 4 down to No. 200, while the sedimentation cylinder settles silt and clay fractions over a 24-hour reading schedule. Chandler’s basin-fill geology — a mix of coarse alluvial fan gravels near the San Tan Mountains and fine-grained playa silts along the Price Road Corridor — demands dual-method testing. A single sieve run misses the sub-75-micron fraction that controls drainage and shrink-swell behavior. We run dispersing agent checks on every hydrometer batch because the calcareous fines common in Maricopa County can flocculate in distilled water, skewing the clay reading. Combined with Atterberg limits testing, the gradation curve gives engineers the full picture for foundation design.

Gravel dominates Chandler’s east-side fans — but it’s the 12% silt fraction that governs drainage, and that number lives in the hydrometer reading, not the sieve stack.

How we work

Chandler’s summer heat and monsoon-season moisture swings alter sample preparation. Lab air-drying at 110°F ambient can bake clay aggregates into pseudo-sand particles, so our team uses a 60°C oven cap and regular disaggregation checks during the 24-hour pre-dry. The hydrometer analysis runs under ASTM D7928 with a 152H instrument calibrated to Chandler’s municipal water hardness — dissolved solids here shift the meniscus correction by nearly half a division. We report percent gravel, sand, silt, and clay with D10, D30, D60, and the coefficients of uniformity and curvature. For Chandler projects near the Loop 202 Santan Freeway expansion, where imported fill must meet MAG Specification Section 310, the gradation report doubles as a compaction predictor, feeding directly into Proctor testing for maximum dry density correlation. Every curve includes the USCS group symbol and a plain-language description the contractor can read on-site.

Local geotechnical context

A three-story medical office on Chandler Heights Road was deferred by the city plan reviewer because the geotech report classified the site soil as silty sand based on visual-manual description alone. The contractor had already staked the footing layout. We mobilized a next-day sample pickup, ran the full sieve-and-hydrometer suite, and delivered a USCS curve showing the material was actually a low-plasticity clayey sand — SC, not SM. The difference changed the allowable bearing pressure by 600 psf under IBC Table 1806.2. Chandler’s older agricultural parcels west of Arizona Avenue contain buried irrigation berms of reworked clay that look like native sandy loam in a hand-auger cut. Without the hydrometer step, those clay lenses go unnoticed. The risk isn’t theoretical — it’s a pad that passes a proof-roll in June and heaves in August after the first monsoon soak.

Questions and answers

How much does a grain size analysis with hydrometer cost in Chandler?

A combined sieve-and-hydrometer package typically runs between US$100 and US$220 per sample depending on volume and whether the material is predominantly granular or fine-grained. Rush reporting, field sampling by our crew, or multiple splits from the same boring add incremental charges. We quote firm pricing after seeing the drill logs or hand-auger descriptions.

Why do I need the hydrometer if the soil looks sandy?

Because the fraction passing the No. 200 sieve — visible only in the hydrometer — controls drainage, frost susceptibility, and the USCS group symbol. A sand with 12% fines classifies as SM or SC, with different shear strength and compaction behavior than a clean sand. Chandler basin-fill deposits frequently carry 8 to 20 percent silt and clay that the eye misses.

How long does the hydrometer test take and can it be expedited?

The full sedimentation reading schedule spans a minimum of 24 hours after mixing and dispersing. We cannot shorten the physics — clay settles at about 0.001 mm per second. Standard reporting is three working days from sample receipt, and we offer a 24-hour rush on the sieve portion with the hydrometer curve issued the following morning after the final reading.
